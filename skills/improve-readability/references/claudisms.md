# Claudisms

Source: [Claudisms](https://claudisms.ai/), fetched 2026-11-01. The page is a living editorial watchlist with no displayed publication date or version.

This note summarizes its advice, not its full catalog. The source's linked references have not been independently reviewed. Its preferences are not validated tests of AI authorship.

## Editorial checks

1. Cut empty setup. Inspect phrases such as "the point is", "it's worth noting", and "at its core". State the observation instead of announcing its importance or narrating its discovery.

2. Replace vague or inflated wording. Inspect "leverage", "the risk lives in", and "transformative". Use the supported action, relationship, or measured effect. Do not invent details to sound concrete. Match intensity and rankings to evidence.

3. Review sentence patterns. Repeated "not X, but Y" contrasts, dramatic discovery stories, and formulaic conclusions can distract. Check for monotonous short sentences and repeated interruptions. Preserve distinctions that help the reader.

4. Remove invented testimony. Personal reactions, career histories, claims about "most people", and recurring observations need support in the input. Delete unsupported claims or flag missing evidence; softer wording does not make them true.

5. Check formatting and dialogue. The source rejects em dashes, emoji, horizontal-rule dividers, bold-label lists, and consultant-style summaries in its preferred article style. For spoken scripts, it also flags repeated points across speakers and artificial filler words.

## How to apply it

Use these as contextual checks unless the user requests strict Claudisms style. Reader-first requirements take precedence over conflicting house-style preferences.

1. Preserve quotations, code, URLs, identifiers, and precise terms. A watched word may be correct in its literal or technical sense.
2. Keep required structure and supported contrasts. A decision memo can need grouped arguments and a review footer even when an article would not.
3. Do not blindly replace "essay" with "article", a mathematical "real", or financial "compound". The source both bans "compound" broadly and later permits genuine compounding; retain precision and flag that conflict in a strict audit.
4. Strict style changes editable prose, not facts or protected material. Avoiding watched patterns proves neither accuracy nor human authorship.

## Examples

| "The deployment was seamless: no requests failed." | "No requests failed during deployment." |
